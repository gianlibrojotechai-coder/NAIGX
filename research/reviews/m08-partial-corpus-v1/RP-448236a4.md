## 1 · The submitted input

```text
Compliance certificate tracking for our lettings portfolio

We manage 1,400 tenancies across the North West on behalf of about 300 landlords.
Every property needs a set of certificates kept current, and getting this wrong is
not a paperwork problem - it can invalidate a Section 21 notice and in the worst
case it is a criminal offence for the landlord.

What we track per property: Gas Safety Record (annual), EICR (five-yearly), EPC
(ten-yearly), and where applicable a legionella risk assessment and an HMO licence
with its own expiry.

How it works now. Certificates live as PDFs in a folder structure on SharePoint,
named inconsistently. Expiry dates are typed into a column in our property management
system (Reapit). A property manager runs a report every Monday filtered on the next
60 days, emails the landlord to request booking, and chases weekly. When the
certificate comes back they file the PDF and update the date by hand.

Where it goes wrong. The Monday report only catches what was typed in correctly -
we found 40 properties last year with a blank expiry field, which means they were
never chased at all. Landlords ignore emails; there is no escalation beyond the
property manager remembering. And when a certificate is renewed, nothing checks that
the PDF filed actually matches the property or the date entered.

What we want. Expiry tracked reliably rather than by data entry, escalating chase
sequences that do not depend on someone remembering, and a check that a filed
certificate corresponds to the property and date recorded against it. A dashboard
showing exposure by landlord would help us have better conversations with them.

Constraints. Reapit is fixed - it is the system of record and we are not changing it.
It has an API but our licence tier limits us to read access without an upgrade we
have not costed. Contractors who issue certificates send them by email as PDFs, in
whatever format they like, and we cannot dictate that. Our team is six property
managers and one office manager; there is no IT function.
```
